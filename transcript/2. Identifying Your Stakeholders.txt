[Video description begins] Topic title: Identifying Your Stakeholders. [Video description ends]

Departments that deliver technology-based services and solutions can have a large impact on any organization. A lot of people are interested in and affected by their performance, including people within those organizations and people external to it, such as customers as well as the users of the services. As a result, they have a large number of stakeholders. But what exactly is a stakeholder? A stakeholder is a person with a key interest in or concern with your technology products, services, and projects. They are interested in getting things done, and they must often rely on the solutions that the technology teams provide. The two main types of stakeholder are active stakeholders and decision makers.

Active stakeholders are directly involved in a project. Their decisions and actions regularly impact the project. Examples of active stakeholders include project team members and consultants. They may also be users and customers of IT products or services. Decision makers are not as directly involved in the detail of the project. However, their decisions can influence how the project progresses. Typical decision makers include department managers or project sponsors who provide the funding and resources for a project. Whatever their level, you need ongoing stakeholder support to keep your IT project on track. You must make it clear to your stakeholders how your project enables them to meet their own goals. And you need to deliver value to your customers and users. In a sense, you need to "market" your project, showing how it addresses the needs of the business.

Ongoing communication is essential in this effort. Frequently liaising with stakeholders builds rapport with them and establishes your, and your project's credibility. You become a valuable resource to your stakeholders, and they are more likely to "buy-in" to your project when you communicate results to them, and they can see ongoing progress. The more buy-in you earn for your project, the greater support you build among your stakeholders. And the more stakeholder support you have, the more likely you are to achieve your project goals. However, you need to be careful that you are communicating with the right stakeholders. In other words, you have to identify which stakeholders are most relevant to your project and its success. And then make them aware of what your project is aiming to achieve. Take time to analyze which stakeholders are supporting your efforts and their influence on your project.

There are a number of key considerations to bear in mind when engaging with your stakeholders, whether they are members of your organization or customers. The first is to effectively identify them. This usually occurs at the start of the project. A useful approach is to categorize or segment them according to their roles in your project. Stakeholders can play one of four roles. Some are responsible for doing project work. These would include members of your project team. Other stakeholders are accountable for project outcomes. These would include relevant department managers and project sponsors.

Next, you have stakeholders who are consulted during the project. The most obvious examples of these would be external experts and consultants. In some projects, potential customers or users might also play a consultation role. After all, these are often the people who will ultimately use the project's deliverables. Finally, you have stakeholders who need to be informed about project progress. These would include managers who are not directly involved in the project, but are interested in its success. Bear in mind that identifying stakeholders is not always a straightforward process. You could run into a number of challenges.

One is simply missing stakeholders. In other words, you might fail to identify some key stakeholders. Or, you may make the mistake of targeting too many stakeholders. If you do this, you may end up wasting effort trying to please too many people all the time. Another common challenge is targeting the wrong stakeholders. For example, you may overestimate someone's interest in or influence on your project. So, what can you do, if you face these challenges when you're trying to identify the stakeholders of your project? First, consider missing stakeholders.

Consult your existing stakeholders and see whether they identify any other potential stakeholders. A key question to ask is, "Who cares?" In other words, who else is likely to be concerned about the outcome of this project? Targeting too many stakeholders can be as big a problem as targeting too few. If you find yourself getting overwhelmed by the volume of stakeholders, start grouping the stakeholders according to type. Then you can target stakeholders on a group basis. Be sure to prioritize those groups who have the most influence on your project. The third challenge is targeting the wrong stakeholders.

Remember, not all stakeholders have the same level of influence on your project. So try to better understand your stakeholders. To do this, you need to carefully listen to what they say and observe what they do. Are they engaged and enthusiastic? Or are they bored and negative? Target those who are engaged because they are most likely to buy in to your project. If you have unengaged stakeholders, but you need their input or approval, then you need to make efforts to find out what's behind their lack of interest and work with them directly to try and re-engage them. By identifying your key stakeholders and actively engaging with them, you can help ensure the success of your tech project.
